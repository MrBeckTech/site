Words to Balanced Equation

SCIENCE 1206 · UNIT 2

Turn a sentence about a reaction into a balanced chemical equation, one small step at a time.

IN PLAIN WORDS

A word equation tells you about a reaction in a sentence. A chemical equation says the same thing in chemistry shorthand.

It is a recipe: what you start with → what you make. The sentence always tells you what is made. You never have to guess.

Clue words do the work. "Reacts with" or "and" means +. "Produces", "yields" or "forms" means →.

FIVE SMALL STEPS

1 Find the substances. 2 Write each formula. 3 Build the equation. 4 Balance it. 5 Name the reaction type.

FORMULA REMINDERS

Alone, these come in pairs (HONClBrIF): H2, O2, N2, Cl2, Br2, I2, F2. Phosphorus is P4 and sulfur is S8.

In a compound, the pair is gone. Potassium chloride is KCl, not KCl2. Use the charges.

Roman numerals give the metal's charge. Iron(III) means Fe3+. Copper(I) sulfate is Cu2SO4, because two Cu+ balance one SO42−.

REACTION TYPES

Check them in this order:

  1. A hydrocarbon + O2 → CO2 + H2O? Combustion.
  2. Only one reactant? Decomposition.
  3. Only one product? Formation (also called synthesis).
  4. A lone element on each side? Single replacement.
  5. Two compounds → two compounds? Double replacement.

🔒 SUBSCRIPTS ARE LOCKED

You wrote these formulas in step 2. Changing a small number now would make a different substance. For example, H2O is water but H2O2 is hydrogen peroxide.

Change coefficients, never subscripts.
