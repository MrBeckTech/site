IN PLAIN WORDS

When an acid and a base meet, the H+ from the acid and the OH− from the base grab each other and become water (H2O).

So both teams get weaker, and the pH heads toward 7. This is called neutralization.

The other ions are left over and make a salt. So: acid + base → salt + water.

NEUTRALIZATION

THE PATTERN

acid + base → salt + water

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O

It is a double replacement: the H from the acid and the metal from the base swap partners.

WHY THE PH MOVES

Each OH− you add uses up one H+, so the acid gets weaker and the pH rises toward 7.

The pH is exactly 7 only when the amounts match. Add more base after that, and the extra OH− pushes the pH past 7.

CARBONATES

Carbonates, like the CaCO3 in antacids and limestone, also neutralize acids. They make a salt, water and carbon dioxide gas. That's the fizz.
