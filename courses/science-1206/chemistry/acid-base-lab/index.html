IN PLAIN WORDS

Acids and bases are two opposite teams. In water, acids bring H+ (hydrogen ions). Bases bring OH− (hydroxide ions).

The pH scale is a "sour-to-slippery" ruler from 0 to 14, with 7 in the middle. Below 7 is acidic (like sour lemon juice). 7 is neutral (pure water). Above 7 is basic (like slippery soap).

We never taste or touch chemicals to test them. We use indicators instead: they change colour.

THE RULES

H first (like HCl) → acid. Metal + OH (like NaOH) → base. Metal + another ion (like NaCl) → salt. Water and other molecular compounds (like CH4) are none of these.

INDICATORS

Litmus: blue litmus turns red in acid. Red litmus turns blue in base. Near neutral, neither changes.

Phenolphthalein: colourless in acids and neutral solutions. Pink above about pH 8.2.

Universal indicator / pH paper: a colour for each pH: red (acid) → green (7) → purple (base).

Red cabbage juice: red or pink in acid, purple near neutral, green or yellow in base.

pH meter: gives the exact number.

WHY BOTH CONDUCT

Acids and bases both make ions in water: H+ or OH−. Ions carry electric charge, so the solutions conduct electricity.

Salt water conducts too, because salt makes ions. Distilled water has almost no ions, so it barely conducts.

REMEMBER

The pH values here are approximate. Real lemon juice, for example, can be anywhere from about 2 to 2.6.

Most acids react with metals like magnesium, giving off hydrogen gas. Most bases don't.
