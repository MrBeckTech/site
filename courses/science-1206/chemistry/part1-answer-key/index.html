SDS = Safety Data Sheet

Tells you: Hazards associated with a substance, how to handle, store, and dispose of it safely, and first aid procedures if something goes wrong.

Interpreting an SDS (Copper(II) Sulfate)

  1. Section 1 – Identification of Substance:
    • a) Trade Name: Copper(II) Sulfate (or Cupric Sulfate)
    • b) Producing Company: Cayman Chemical Company
  2. Section 2 – Hazard(s) Identification:
    • a) Aquatic Acute 1 description: Very toxic to aquatic life.
    • b) 2 Associated WHMIS Symbols: Exclamation Mark (Health Hazard / Irritant) & Environment (Aquatic Toxicity)
    • c) 3 Hazard Statements: H302: Harmful if swallowed. | H315: Causes skin irritation. | H319: Causes serious eye irritation.
    • d) 3 Precautionary Statements: P264: Wash thoroughly after handling. | P273: Avoid release to the environment. | P280: Wear protective gloves and eye protection.
  3. Section 4 – First Aid Measures:
    • a) Ingestion: Rinse mouth with water. Do NOT induce vomiting unless directed by medical personnel. Get immediate medical attention.
    • b) Inhalation: Remove person to fresh air. If not breathing, give artificial respiration and get medical attention.
    • c) Skin Contact: Wash thoroughly with plenty of soap and water. Remove contaminated clothing.
    • d) Eye Contact: Rinse cautiously with water for several minutes. Remove contact lenses if present and easy to do. Continue rinsing.
  4. Section 5 – Fire Fighting Measures:
    • Suitable extinguishing agents: Use water spray, alcohol-resistant foam, dry chemical, or carbon dioxide (CO₂).

3. Ionic & Molecular Fundamentals

Ionic Compound

  • Metal (or NH₄⁺) + Non-metal (or Polyatomic Anion)
  • Cation: Positively charged ion formed by losing electrons (usually metals)
  • Anion: Negatively charged ion formed by gaining electrons (non-metals end in -ide)
  • Examples: NaCl, CaCO₃, KMnO₄
  • Ionic Bond: A bond where valence electrons are TRANSFERRED; attraction between oppositely charged ions.

Molecular Compound

  • Non-metal element + Non-metal element
  • Examples: C₃H₈, N₂O, CO₂, CH₄
  • Covalent Bond: A bond where atoms SHARE valence electrons.
  • Molecule: Two or more non-metal atoms joined together by covalent bonds.

Diatomic & Special Polyatomic Elements

  • Diatomic Molecules: Two atoms of the same element, chemically bonded, forming a molecular substance.
  • Remember: HONClBrIF(Say Honkle brif) Elements: H₂, O₂, N₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂ (and At₂)
  • Special Polyatomic Elements: Phosphorus (P₄), Sulfur (S₈)

4. Writing & Naming Molecular Compounds

Writing Formulas Rules

  1. Write each atom symbol.
  2. Each prefix indicates the number of atoms present (written as subscript).
  3. If no prefix is present on the second element, assume 1 atom. (RULE: "mono-" is NEVER used for the first atom!).

Naming Rules for Molecular Substances

  1. First element is named in full (farthest left on periodic table).
  2. Second element name is shortened and given an "-ide" ending.
  3. Use prefixes to indicate the number of each kind of atom.
  4. The prefix "mono-" is never used for the first element.
  5. Drop double vowels (e.g., monooxide → monoxide, pentaoxide → pentoxide).
