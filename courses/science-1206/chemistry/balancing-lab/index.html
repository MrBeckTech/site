Count the atoms on both sides, then change the big numbers until they match.

IN PLAIN WORDS

A chemical equation is a recipe. It shows what you start with → what you make.

Atoms are like LEGO bricks. A reaction takes the bricks apart and builds something new. No bricks are lost, and no new bricks appear.

So both sides must have the same bricks. Balancing means counting the bricks on each side until they match.

THE SANDWICH RULE

The coefficient (the big number in front) is how many sandwiches you make. The subscript (the small low number) is what is inside one sandwich.

You can make more sandwiches, but you can't change the sandwich.

WHY BALANCE?

In a chemical reaction, atoms are not made and not destroyed. They just join up in new ways. This is the law of conservation of mass: the total mass before a reaction equals the total mass after.

A balanced equation shows this. Every kind of atom has the same count on both sides of the arrow.

HOW TO BALANCE

  1. Count each kind of atom on each side.
  2. Change coefficients, never subscripts.
  3. Start with the biggest formula. Leave lone elements (like Fe or O2) for last.
  4. Burning a fuel (combustion)? Go C, then H, then O.
  5. A polyatomic ion (like NO3) that is the same on both sides? Count the polyatomic ion as one unit.
  6. Check every element. Use the smallest whole numbers. A coefficient of 1 is not written.

THE ODD–EVEN TRICK

O2 always brings oxygen in pairs (2, 4, 6 …). If the other side needs an odd number of O, no whole number in front of O2 works.

Fix: double every coefficient. Now the O is even, and you can finish.

Example: iron rusting, Fe + O2 → Fe2O3

  1. Fe: 2 on the right, so 2Fe + O2 → Fe2O3
  2. O: 3 on the right (odd). O2 gives 2 or 4, never 3. It would take 1½ O2, and coefficients must be whole numbers.
  3. Double every coefficient (2, 1½, 1 become 4, 3, 2): 4Fe + 3O2 → 2Fe2O3
  4. Check: Fe 4 = 4. O 6 = 6. Balanced.

Elements that come in pairs when they are alone: HONClBrIF = H2, O2, N2, Cl2, Br2, I2, F2.

🔒 SUBSCRIPTS ARE LOCKED

A subscript tells you what is inside one molecule. Change it, and you have made a different substance.

H2O
water
H2O2
hydrogen peroxide (used as a bleach)

Changing H2O to H2O2 turns water into hydrogen peroxide. Then you are not balancing the same reaction any more.

Change coefficients, never subscripts. Add more whole molecules with the big number in front instead.
