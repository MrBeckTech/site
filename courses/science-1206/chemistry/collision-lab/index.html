Reaction rates: what makes a reaction faster or slower? Change one thing at a time and watch the collisions.

IN PLAIN WORDS

Particles only react when they crash into each other. Think of bumper cars.

More cars, faster cars, or more open space to get hit, and you get more crashes.

But a crash only "counts" if it is hard enough and hits the right spot. In this lab, the right spot is the gold sticky side on each particle.

The reaction rate is how fast the reaction happens: how many new particles are made each second.

REACTION RATES IN REAL LIFE

COLLISION THEORY

Particles react only when they collide:

  1. often enough (more collisions per second),
  2. with enough energy (the energy needed is called the activation energy),
  3. and in the right orientation (the right spot).

THE FIVE FACTORS

Temperature: faster particles hit more often and harder.

Concentration: more particles in the same space hit more often.

Surface area: smaller pieces have more particles on the outside to hit.

Catalyst: lowers the energy needed. It doesn't make particles collide more often, and it isn't used up.

Light: gives energy to particles, but only in light-sensitive reactions.

WHY IT SLOWS DOWN

Watch the rate graph: the reaction is fastest at the start. As reactants get used up, there are fewer of them to collide, so the rate drops.
