1. LAW OF CONSERVATION OF MASS

1. In a chemical reaction, mass is neither created nor destroyed. The total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products.

2. The mass of the reactants and the mass of the products are equal (the same).

3. Use mass of reactants = mass of products. The equations are not balanced, and you don't need to balance them: mass in = mass out no matter what. The missing mass is shown as ?.

8. MORE UNIT 2 PRACTICE QUESTIONS

  1. 2+. Losing 2 electrons (each −1) leaves 2 more protons than electrons. Symbol: X2+.
  2. 2+. Sulfide is S2−. The charges in a compound must add to zero, so if one unknown ion balances one S2−, it must be 2+.
  3. Magnesium (12 p+) is 2-8-2. It loses its 2 outer electrons to become [Mg]2+, 2-8. Diagram: draw the atom with 2, 8, 2 electrons, an arrow labelled "loses 2 e−", then the ion in square brackets with 2 and 8 electrons and 2+ at the top right.
  4. Double replacement (two compounds swap partners): 2LiOH + MgCl2 → 2LiCl + Mg(OH)2.
  5. Combustion. The "other gases" (such as carbon monoxide) mean it is incomplete combustion: there was not enough oxygen.
  6. In ionic compounds the charges decide the ratio, so there is only one possible formula (e.g., NaCl). Two non-metals can combine in more than one ratio (CO and CO2), so prefixes are needed to say which one.
  7. Acids (any three): sour, turn blue litmus red, react with most metals, pH less than 7, produce H+ in water, conduct electricity. Bases (any three): bitter, slippery, turn red litmus blue, pH greater than 7, produce OH− in water, conduct electricity.
  8. Any two: raise the temperature, increase the concentration, increase the surface area (smaller pieces), add a catalyst (or light, for light-sensitive reactions).
