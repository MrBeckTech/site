Sig Fig Practice

SCIENCE 1206 · UNIT 3

Count significant figures, round, and use the right rule for adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing.

IN PLAIN WORDS

Sig figs are the digits you actually measured. A calculator spits out lots of digits, but it can't make your ruler any better.

So you round the answer to match the measurements you started with.

Every measurement is all the digits you are sure of, plus one estimated digit. A ruler reading of 67.85 cm has 4 sig figs: the 6, 7 and 8 come from the marks, and the 5 is the guess between two marks.

RULES FOR COUNTING SIGNIFICANT FIGURES

RULEEXAMPLES
1. Non-zero digits (1–9) are always significant.34 → 2 SF   4257 → 4 SF   1.74 → 3 SF
2. Zeros can be significant or not. It depends on where they are:
A. Middle zeros (between non-zero digits) ARE significant.507 → 3 SF   5009 → 4 SF
B. Leading zeros (in front of the first non-zero digit) are NOT significant.0.0015 → 2 SF   0.0108 → 3 SF
C. Trailing zeros (at the end) are significant ONLY if the number has a decimal point.520.0 → 4 SF   0.01010 → 4 SF
1500 → 2 SF   10800 → 3 SF

Leading zeros are just placeholders that show how small a number is (0.0015 m = 1.5 mm, still only 2 digits measured). In scientific notation, every digit in the front number is significant: 1.50 × 103 has 3 SF.

ROUNDING

If the digit to the right of the last digit you keep is 5 or more, round up (add 1 to the last kept digit).

If it is 4 or less, do not round up (the last kept digit stays the same).

"5 or more, let it soar. 4 or less, let it rest."

Example: 0.0037421 to 3 SF

  1. Find the first significant digit and count over: 0.00374|21
  2. Look at the very next digit: 2
  3. 2 is 4 or less, so leave the 4 alone and drop the rest: 0.00374 (keep the leading zeros as placeholders!)

ADDING AND SUBTRACTING

Round the answer to the same precision as the least precise measurement. For decimals, that means the fewest decimal places.

An answer can only be as precise as the least precise measurement you started with. A chain is only as strong as its weakest link.

Watch out: whole numbers ending in zero, like 320. The 0 is not significant, so 320 is only known to the tens place. 320 + 12.5 = 332.5, and only the tens place can be trusted → 330 (3.3 × 102). So the full rule is: round to the least precise place value.

MULTIPLYING AND DIVIDING

The answer has the same number of significant figures as the measurement with the fewest significant figures.

Don't mix up the rules!
Adding / subtracting → count decimal places.
Multiplying / dividing → count significant figures.

Exact numbers don't limit sig figs. Counted numbers and defined conversion factors are exact: 28 students, 1 km = 1000 m, 60 s = 1 min, 3.6.

50.0 km/h ÷ 3.6 = 13.888… The 3.6 is exact, so only 50.0 (3 SF) counts → 13.9 m/s (not 14 m/s!).

Round only at the end. In a problem with more than one step, keep all the calculator digits until the final answer. Then round once.
